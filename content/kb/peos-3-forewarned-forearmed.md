## Stay away from illegal recruiters

There will always be people who will try to lure you into what would appear to be an easier way to get a job abroad. Be very careful! Stay away from them!

A person, company or entity without a license or authority from the POEA who does any of the following is an illegal recruiter: C - Canvass, E - Enlist, T - Transport, C - Contract, H - Hire, U - Utilize, P - Procure workers for employment abroad whether for profit or otherwise.

It also includes the following: C - contract services, R - referring, P - promising, A - advertising for employment abroad.

## Be a documented OFW

As your future - as well as your loved ones - is at stake, you need to be vigilant at all times. You must exert due diligence every step of the way. Do not be misled or influenced by mere hearsay; be critical of what you read. Seek confirmation from the POEA itself.

Deal only with licensed recruitment agencies; they are the only ones authorized to recruit and deploy workers. An agency without a license means it is an illegal recruiter!

## How you can be safe from illegal recruiters

1. Make sure the licensed agency has a POEA approved job order.
2. Deal only with authorized representatives of a licensed agency; an ID is not enough. Licensed agencies are required to post their organizational charts as well as the names and photos of their officers and employees.
3. Transact business only in the registered office address of the licensed agency; be sure the publicized office and the office you are going to are one and the same.
4. Pay only the maximum allowable placement fee of one month of the basic salary, exclusive of documentation cost. But collection of such fees from seafarers and household service workers (HSWs) are prohibited. There are countries like the United Kingdom, Ireland, Norway, the Netherlands, and the provinces of Canada: Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, and British Columbia that do not allow collection of placement fees.
5. Pay the placement fee after signing the Employment Contract; demand an official receipt (OR).
6. Do not send money by money transfer or remittance of any form just to receive application forms or to process your papers in exchange of a job abroad.
7. When offered a tourist or visit visa to work abroad, turn it down. A legal job offer means a visa or work permit will be issued you.
8. Deal directly with government offices; avoid fixers!
9. Memorize: www.poea.gov.ph

## Tactics of illegal recruiters: advertisements, correspondence and tourist visas

Know the modus operandi of illegal recruiters. Here are some of the tactics they use to mislead and extract hard-earned money from job applicants.

- **Blind Advertisements:** Without completely identifying themselves, recruiters advertise job openings in newspapers asking applicants to send in their papers via a P.O. Box address, and a minimal processing fee. You will never know for a fact what they advertise is for real. There is no way you can check this! Chances are they will continue to ask more money to continue with the "processing" of your papers!
- **Correspondence:** Like the blind advertisements, you are asked to submit your job application through the mail together with a minimal processing fee. Interested applicants are encouraged to submit the requirements for application through mail together with a seemingly minimal fee. Don't be misled!
- **Tourist Visa, Worker Talaga!:** Illegally recruited workers leave the country as tourists to work abroad. Without proper documentation, workers who fall for this trap are open to abuse and cannot report cases of maltreatment because they are working illegally. Worst of all, you cannot enforce the contract in court. Do things right! Go through the legal process the POEA provides and enjoy enormous benefits you are entitled to.

## Tactics of illegal recruiters: escorts, backdoor exits and forged documents

- **Escort Services:** Unscrupulous persons escort workers to the airports and stealthily board the plane without the required travel documents. When they can no longer back out, the workers will be forced to pay hundreds of thousands of pesos as facilitation fee.
- **Backdoor Exit:** Illegally recruited workers leave the country through the southern ports of exit where immigration control may be lax. They board cargo ships or boats. Once abroad they are on their own - no one to protect them from employers' abuses, no benefits to enjoy.
- **Forged Documents:** Illegal recruiters facilitate the departure of workers from the Philippines using falsified documents, i.e., birth certificate, "baklas" passports; illegal recruiters use this to deploy minors.
- **Direct Hire:** In this type of illegal activity, an employer hires Filipino job applicants without going through POEA or Philippine licensed agencies. The law prohibits direct hiring.

## Tactics of illegal recruiters: trainee schemes, tie-ups and consultancies

- **Trainee-Worker Scheme:** Under the pretext that they are going to be trained abroad, applicants are asked to sign a Training Agreement with the recruiter but actually work in hotels/restaurants in a foreign country where they are just given allowances or paid salaries way below those given regular employees.
- **Tie-up or Kabit System (Repro-scheme):** Unlicensed agencies recruit workers using the facilities of a licensed recruiter: a worker is processed using the job order of a licensed agency. Upon reaching the country of destination, the actual job given is not the actual job stated in the job order. For example the papers of a household worker is processed as nurse, saleslady or janitress.
- **Visa Assistance or Immigration Consultancy:** There are some companies that operate as consultants to guide applicants on how to obtain an immigrant visa; this is legal. But offering a job overseas is illegal. Always be on guard!
- **Seminars/Sports Events/Conferences:** Illegal recruiters facilitate the participation of applicants to well-known seminars, sports events or conferences abroad, then stay to work there.
- **Mail Order Bride Scheme:** Recruiters arrange the marriage between Filipino women and foreigners. Once in the country of destination, the "wife" ends up as a domestic helper and suffers immeasurably!

## Tactics of illegal recruiters: online scams

- **Online Scam:** Spam and scam rhyme - be very careful when receiving notices of job availabilities abroad! Do not give information freely about yourself or other data that scammers may use to develop a very tempting job offer.
- **Internet Scam:** Advertisement and recruitment of workers for overseas employment through the internet which are meant to defraud, traffic or smuggle workers. Various internet users have complained of having been victimized by bogus websites that require applicants to immediately remit money through money transfer services for intended visa, airfare and processing fees. Watch for:
  - Sending appointment letters to applicant workers and asking them to send money to travel agencies to arrange their travel requirements.
  - Use of mobile phone or email as a means to contact the alleged employer/agent (scammer) without giving any landline number, or if there is one, it cannot be contacted.
  - Designation of unauthorized third parties or agents, and in some cases alleged immigration officers, to process their work permit applications and visas.
  - Use of scanned recruitment documents such as special power of attorney, recruitment agreement, manpower request, and business or registration papers.
  - Giving fake websites that purportedly contain employer's information and other details which are expectedly incorrect.

The bottom line is to immediately check and confirm with the POEA. Remember, an agency without a POEA license is an illegal recruiter without a conscience!

## Tactics of illegal recruiters: travel covers

- **Educational Tours:** Victims recruited will travel in a group of two or more as students. The recruiter will prepare the documents to be presented at the airport that include school credentials to prove that they are students.
- **Religious Pilgrimage:** Usually traveling as a group and presenting credentials of their religious affiliations supposedly to go for a pilgrimage but with the end purpose of working upon reaching their destination.
- **Detached Visa:** Worker intending to work in South Korea under E-6.2 Visa (Entertainer Visa issued by Korean Embassy) misrepresents by travelling either as a tourist or worker to the Middle East but with connecting flight to an Asian country. The visa is detached from the passport for purposes of departing from the Philippines but attaches the same visa upon reaching transit, and boards onward flight to Korea, circumventing the POEA requirements for OFW in destination country.
- **NAIA Terminal 3 Open Market:** Recruiters at NAIA Terminal 3 are waiting for off-loaded passengers and offering them immediate deployment bragging about their connections with Immigration Officers.
- **Wedding/Grand Occasion Invitation:** Passengers traveling to attend a fictitious ceremony, carrying complete apparel and formal printed invitation.
- **"Syota" System:** Two persons, a male and a female, having no relation with each other, pose as lovers and tourists on a leisure trip but they will separate upon reaching their destination. They will go to their final destination. Only one of them will return to the Philippines but there is a possibility that both of them would not.
- **"Yaya" Method:** A minor child who will travel to see her parent abroad is accompanied by her "yaya". The yaya possesses necessary documents including the required clearance signed by the parent. Afterwards, the child will return to the Philippines accompanied by her parent and the yaya left behind.

## Beware: your recruiter may be a drug trafficker

The emerging trend in transporting illegal drugs using "drug couriers" has become a worldwide concern because it poses serious threats to health, it violates human rights and it cultivates illegal activities and other crimes such as trafficking and prostitution.

Human traffickers and illegal recruiters have joined forces with drug traffickers to target prospective OFWs especially those heading for Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) member states that have a "no tourist visa" pact with the Philippines. These unsuspecting OFWs are being used as drug couriers, often deceived by their recruiters about their real jobs or the conditions of their employment overseas.

## Modus operandi of drug traffickers

- Couriers are usually recruited by casual acquaintances they meet in the Philippines or abroad, mostly fellow Filipinos connected to drug syndicates like the African Drug Syndicate (ADS), offering plane tickets, hotel accommodation and huge amounts of money.
- Members of syndicates befriend/marry potential recruits then later turn him/her into a courier or cohort.
- Couriers may also be recruited through the internet and social networking sites like Facebook, MySpace, Tagged, etc.
- Drug traffickers can also turn their recruits into seasoned drug mules by having them repeatedly carry drugs in exchange for a big amount of money.
- Drug traffickers pose as recruiters offering jobs mostly in non-visa countries such as in ASEAN member states - Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, Brunei, and Thailand, but later duped them into carrying packages, not knowing that drugs were placed inside.
- Drug traffickers who pose as recruiters also lend their bags to the recruits or request recruits to bring a package as "pasalubong" for someone.
- Unwitting victims are duped by acquaintances into carrying packages in exchange for money, not knowing that the package has illegal drugs or substances placed inside.
- Syndicates also engage in travel & tours businesses/agencies to arrange airline and hotel bookings of the couriers and use fraudulent documents/fake credit cards.

## How to avoid being a drug courier

- Make sure that you are dealing with a licensed recruiter, and that you have a contract with a definite employer.
- Even if certain countries do not require visas for tourists, a work visa or permit is still required in order to work in these countries.
- Be vigilant on people offering easy money and work abroad especially when you are unsure of the nature of the job.
- Avoid bringing other people's luggage/packages especially when you do not know what is contained inside. Do not trust anybody, even acquaintances, when you are traveling abroad. A big amount of money is not worth it.

## Report illegal recruiters

The bottom line is to immediately check and confirm any job offer with the POEA. Remember, an agency without a POEA license is an illegal recruiter without a conscience!

Do not fall for offers promising an easier way to go abroad. As a documented worker, you are entitled to the government's full protection. Enjoy your rights, privileges, and benefits.

Should you encounter an illegal recruiter, report him or her to POEA. Go to our Operations and Surveillance Division at the 4th floor, Blas F. Ople Bldg., Mandaluyong City.

The POEA way is definitely the right way!
