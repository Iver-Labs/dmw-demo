## The employment contract

After being pre-selected for the job and knowing the fees to pay, you are just about ready to get a copy of the Employment Contract. Study its contents.

Since you will bind yourself to a legal and enforceable contract in the Philippines and the host country, go over the list of the minimum provisions. Read each one carefully. Seek the assistance of the POEA if you have any concern.

## Minimum provisions: worksite, length, position and work schedule

- **Place of Assignment or Specific Worksite:** The building, street, city/district, and province of your worksite must be clearly stated. This should not be changed upon your arrival in the host country.
- **Length of the Contract:** You are hired for a specific time period; this starts from the date you leave the Philippines and the date you will return. Extending your stay does not mean an automatic adjustment in your salary.
- **Position or Designation:** This is very crucial. Make sure your designation is clearly stated. There are salary rates for specific position or job designation. Make sure you perform the tasks specified in the contracted position.
- **Work Schedule of 8 hours per day and 6 days a week:** As overseas worker, you are required to render 8 hours of work per day, 5 or 6 days a week. Number of rest day/s must be clearly stated in the contract.

## Minimum provisions: salary, overtime, transportation, food and leave

- **Basic Monthly Salary:** This is the amount you will receive for the working hours rendered. Your employer may assist you in remitting a percentage of your salary through the proper banking channel or other means authorized by law.
- **Overtime Pay Rate:** This is the pay you will receive for the work you will be asked to perform over and above the regular 8 hours. Make sure you are familiar with how this is computed.
- **Free transportation to & from worksite:** Your employer must provide you with free transportation going to and returning from the worksite. If the employer will not provide this, make sure there is an equivalent amount to cover for transportation expenses or there are other benefits to compensate.
- **Free food and accommodation:** Your employer must provide you with free food and accommodation while in the worksite. If the employer will not provide this, make sure there is an equivalent amount to cover for food and accommodation expenses or there are other benefits to compensate.
- **Vacation leave, sick leave, medical & dental benefits:** You are entitled to vacation leave and sick leave. Know the number of sick and vacation leaves available to you for the duration of your contract. Your free annual medical and dental examinations must also be included in the contract.

## Minimum provisions: compensation and repatriation of remains

- **Compensation in case of work-related disablement or injury:** Although the compulsory insurance coverage provides compensation for work-related disablement or injury, make sure this is one of the provisions included in your Employment Contract. The amount of compensation must be clearly stated.
- **Repatriation of remains and personal belongings:** The repatriation of remains and personal belongings is the responsibility of the employer. In case repatriation is not possible, arrangements with regard to the remains will be made with the Philippine Embassy/Consulate and/or the next of kin.

## Termination of contract by the employer

In as much as your employer has a list of obligations to fulfill to ensure your productive engagement in the worksite, there are also a number of causes that your services may be terminated. These include:

- serious misconduct
- willful disobedience of employer's lawful orders
- habitual neglect of duties
- absenteeism
- insubordination
- revealing secrets of establishment
- violation of customs, traditions, and laws of the host country and/or terms of the Employment Contract

In this situation, you will shoulder your repatriation expenses.

## Termination of contract by the worker, and illness or injury

You too can terminate your own contract when the employer is at fault. These causes include the following:

- serious insult by the employer or his representative
- inhumane and unbearable treatment accorded the employee by the employer or his representative
- commission of a crime/offense by the employer or his representative
- violation of the terms and conditions of the employment contract by the employer or his representative

In any of these cases, your employer shall shoulder your repatriation expenses.

You may also terminate the contract without just cause by serving one (1) month in advance written notice to the employer. If you fail to serve the one month notice, your employer may hold you liable for damages and you will pay for your repatriation expenses.

In case you get ill or injured, you or your employer may terminate the contract. In this regard, your employer pays for the repatriation expenses.

## Contract signing

Take a deep breath, then sign the Employment Contract!

This signed contract will be processed at the POEA and will be returned to you together with the following:

- Overseas Employment Certificate (OEC)
- Pre-Departure Orientation Seminar (PDOS) Certificate
- Passport
- Visa

One last reminder, do not agree to have these documents given to you only at the airport.
