## PRICEST: what should I consider before deciding to work overseas?

What factors should I consider before deciding to work overseas? Let's take a closer look at the concerns you are faced with when you work abroad.

For your easy reference, the catchword is: "PRICEST"

- P - Physical Mobility
- R - Relationships
- I - Income & Expenses
- C - Career Movement & Direction
- E - Environment
- S - Skill Set
- T - Time

## PRICEST: Physical Mobility

Your ability to move around as you are used to will be limited when you are in the country of your destination.

You may be stationed in a compound with other workers or in a 5 x 10 square meter condominium apartment.

It's going to be mostly doing the tasks assigned you, and not much movement - and free time - as you please.

## PRICEST: Relationships

When you work abroad, you will be leaving your spouse or partner, your children, your parents, friends, and co-employees behind.

You won't be around when birthday or anniversaries are celebrated, or someone close to you is sick or needs your presence.

This can be very stressful. Be ready to cope.

## PRICEST: Income and Expenses

Chances are you will be earning more with your job abroad compared to your salary in the Philippines, but remember expenses also increase proportionately.

Your family may require more financial help from you, aside from paying the personal loans and advances you incurred when you were preparing your trip.

You need to balance income with your expenses.

## PRICEST: Career Movement and Direction

As you are hired for a specific job abroad, you should not expect a promotion to the next higher position in your next posting.

Your assignment to a particular position will most likely remain the same for the entire duration of your employment contract.

Furthermore, the renewal of your contract is solely dependent on your employer.

## PRICEST: Environment

More than the different physical environment - different climate and seasons - of an overseas posting, you will need to adjust to a new culture altogether:

Language so unfamiliar, prescribed type of clothing, different and limited food choices or even taste of food you are not accustomed to, places inaccessible to you, and social relations.

## PRICEST: Skill Set

A number of job openings may interest you - be sure your qualifications as to education and experience (skill set) match with requirements of the job.

If you need to brush up on your know-how and level of experience before signing the employment contract, do so.

Go, when you're truly ready!

## PRICEST: Time

Working abroad means someone else - your employer - specifies the length of time you should accomplish a task.

It is in this sense that "time is not in your hands." But that is one discipline we can all benefit from; our individual productivity increases with the proper use of time!

## PARDA: Important Issues

Alongside "PRICEST" you have to personally confront 5 very important issues -

- P - Preparedness to temporarily leave your family behind
- A - Appropriate qualifications to fill in the position you are applying for
- R - Readiness to work in an unfamiliar, foreign environment
- D - Determination to face new challenges and risks
- A - Availability of money to spend for the job application

Adopting a positive disposition regarding these will certainly pave the way for a clearer and less bumpy road ahead!

After going through all the questions - What do you want? Is your family ready? Are you still going to work abroad or not?
